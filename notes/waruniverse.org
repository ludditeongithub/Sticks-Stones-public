** Rivers and lakes
1. Identify Candidate Basins
   a. For each tile in the map:
       - If the tile is a local minimum (all neighbors higher or equal), mark as potential lake.
   b. Group connected local minima into basins (cluster adjacent low tiles).
   c. For each basin, compute:
       - Basin area = number of connected low tiles
       - Basin depth potential = minimum height difference between basin floor and lowest surrounding rim
   d. Discard basins with depth < minimum threshold (e.g., 1-2 tiles)

2. Compute Water Input for Each Basin
   a. River inflow:
       - Sum volume of all rivers entering the basin (width × flow factor)
   b. Rainfall contribution:
       - rainfall_factor × basin_area
   c. Snowmelt contribution (optional):
       - Add seasonal snowmelt if basin contains mountain/snow tiles
   d. Total water input:
       - water_input = river_inflow + rainfall + snowmelt

3. Determine Lake Surface Area and Depth
   a. Compare water_input to basin capacity (max depth × basin area)
   b. Rules of thumb:
       - Low water_input → lake occupies only deepest tiles → small, deep lake
       - Medium water_input → lake fills most of basin → medium-sized, moderate depth
       - High water_input → lake fills basin, may overflow → large, shallow lake or floodplain

4. Connect Lakes to Rivers
   a. If a river enters the basin:
       - Add its flow to water_input
       - If water_input > basin capacity:
           - Create an outflow river at the lowest rim tile
   b. Ensure water flows logically downstream

5. Optional Seasonal Variation
   a. Wet season:
       - Expand lake into surrounding floodplain tiles
   b. Dry season:
       - Shrink lake, leaving only deepest tiles
   c. Adjust river inflow seasonally if desired

6. Optional Micro-Features
   a. Floodplains: 1–2 tile buffer around large/high-input lakes that fill in wet season
   b. Swamps/marshes: low-lying shallow areas with persistent water
   c. Sediment buildup: edges of lakes can gradually fill over time (optional)

7. Assign Lake Tile Properties
   a. Mark tiles as lake, floodplain, or wetland
   b. Assign depth (optional) based on tile elevation relative to lake water level
   c. Assign micro-features like vegetation, swamps, or marsh tiles

*** 1. Precompute Water Sources
**** Precipitation Map
- Assign each tile a base rainfall value.
- Apply seasonal multipliers:
  - Spring: 1.0
  - Summer: 1.1 (rainy regions) or 0.8 (dry regions)
  - Autumn: 0.6–0.8
  - Winter: 0.3–0.5

**** Snow/Glacier Map
- High-elevation tiles accumulate snow in winter.
- Snowmelt contributes to rivers based on season:
  - Spring: 100%
  - Summer: 20–50%
  - Autumn: 10–20%
  - Winter: 0–10%

**** Groundwater/Baseflow Map (optional)
- Slow, steady contribution from precipitation infiltrating soil.

*** 2. Determine River Sources
- Sources chosen from:
  - Mountain/high-altitude tiles with snow/glacier or high rainfall.
  - Groundwater-fed springs.
- Initial river volume or stage:
  - initial_flow = rainfall + snowmelt + groundwater

*** 3. River Path Generation
- Route rivers along lowest altitude / slope.
- Decide river age:
  - Young rivers → narrow, steep, greedy paths.
  - Mature rivers → wider, meandering, widen tiles along the path.
- Use chunk-based altitude approximation to reduce computation.

*** 4. Volume / Stage Propagation
**** Water Representation
- Float volume (continuous) or
- Discrete stages (integer levels, e.g., 0–10) like Minecraft water blocks.
  - Each stage corresponds to visual width/depth and flow potential.

**** Tile Update Formula
- tile_water = inflow_from_upstream + local_tile_input - evaporation - seepage
- Adjust river width/visuals based on volume/stage.

*** 5. Seasonal Updates
- Apply seasonal multipliers to water input:
  - Snowmelt: Spring 100%, Summer 20–50%, Autumn 10–20%, Winter 0–10%
  - Precipitation: Spring 1.0, Summer 1.1 or 0.8, Autumn 0.6–0.8, Winter 0.3–0.5
- Global seasonal river flow variation:
  - Spring: ~1.0× (peak)
  - Summer: 0.7–0.9×
  - Autumn: 0.6–0.8×
  - Winter: 0–0.5× (snow-fed rivers drop dramatically)
- Snow-fed rivers can vary 2–3× between peak and trough.
- Rain-fed rivers vary according to rainfall seasonality.

*** 6. Lake Generation
- Identify local minima in terrain.
- Determine lake type:
  - Small/floodplain → wet area, no major river.
  - Medium → connected to river, not wet area.
  - Large → connected to river and wet area.
- Update lake volume per season:
  - lake_volume_next = lake_volume_prev + inflow_from_rivers + local_tile_input - outflow - evaporation
- Depth effects:
  - Shallow lakes → fluctuate more, may dry out in dry season.
  - Deep lakes → more stable, smaller relative fluctuations.

*** 7. Optimizations for Tile-Based Games
- Use discrete stages instead of float volumes → reduces computation and simplifies overflow.
- Chunk-based pathfinding for river routing.
- Seasonal updates once per season instead of per frame.
- Overflow/spill: stage exceeds max → spill to adjacent lower tile.
- Width/visuals derived from volume/stage.

*** 8. Tile Water Inputs
- tile_water_input = rainfall + snowmelt + groundwater
- Seasonal multipliers applied.
- Drives initial river volume, propagation, and lake levels.

*** Outcome
- Rivers expand and shrink seasonally.
- Lakes store water and fluctuate realistically.
- Computation remains lightweight for tile-based games.
- Discrete stages simplify logic and rendering.

*** Architecture of Game System
- Cells/regions are spawned by the world spawner, which contain a variable amount of tiles which form various shapes as an entire cell when viewed from a bird's eye view.
  Each region has a biome, altitude, temperature, lakes, and rivers value. 

  They are deterministically spawned using a seed. The same seed is also used to create the vioronoi

* Notes
1. Copper

    Use: Early metal for tools, ornaments, and weapons (often alloyed with tin to create bronze).
    Early Game:
        Surface mining: Found in riverbeds and shallow deposits near the surface, in the form of nuggets or oxide ores.
        Tools: Use copper nuggets or malachite (copper carbonate) in shallow areas.
    Mid Game:
        Copper ore in oxidized rock near surface veins or in copper-rich regions.
        Can be found at shallow to moderate depths (around 10-100 meters).

2. Tin

    Use: Mixed with copper to create bronze (an alloy that revolutionized tools and weapons).
    Early Game:
        Surface mining: Found in alluvial deposits (rivers and stream beds), especially in wetlands.
        Cassiterite (tin oxide) deposits near the surface.
    Mid Game:
        Tin veins in granite rock formations.
        Can be found at moderate depths (around 30-100 meters).

3. Gold

    Use: Used for jewelry, coinage, and as a sign of wealth and status.
    Early Game:
        Panning in riverbeds: Placer gold is the primary form found in rivers and stream beds.
        Gold nuggets can be found in shallow rivers and shallow surface deposits.
    Mid Game:
        Gold veins in quartz or bedrock at moderate depths (10-100 meters).
        Can also be extracted from ancient surface deposits in mountains.

4. Iron

    Use: Widely used in tools, weapons, and construction (starting with early iron age civilizations).
    Early Game:
        Surface mining: Found in the form of iron-rich rocks like hematite, magnetite, and limonite.
        Bog iron can be found in wetlands or rivers where iron deposits have been naturally concentrated.
    Mid Game:
        Iron veins in sedimentary rock layers, typically in mountainous or hilly areas.
        Can be found at moderate to deep depths (50-200 meters), depending on the type of ore.

5. Silver

    Use: Used for coins, jewelry, and ornamentation.
    Early Game:
        Surface mining of silver-bearing ores like argentite in riverbeds.
        Placer deposits where silver is found alongside gold.
    Mid Game:
        Silver veins in mountainous areas or mineralized regions with high metal content.
        Can be found at mid depths (50-100 meters), often near lead or copper deposits.

6. Lead

    Use: Used for pipes, storage, and in alloys (e.g., bronze or glassmaking).
    Early Game:
        Surface mining: Found in riverbeds and shallow deposits of galena (lead sulfide).
    Mid Game:
        Lead veins in sedimentary rock or metamorphic rocks, often alongside silver or zinc.
        Can be found at moderate depths (30-150 meters).

7. Bronze (Alloy of Copper and Tin)

    Use: Revolutionized tool and weapon making; stronger and more durable than pure copper.
    Mid Game:
        Tin and copper are collected separately and then alloyed to create bronze.
        Copper is found at shallow to moderate depths, while tin is found at shallow depths in wetland deposits or surface mines.

8. Zinc

    Use: Used in the creation of brass (an alloy with copper) and for galvanizing.
    Mid Game:
        Zinc ores (e.g., sphalerite) are found in sedimentary rock.
        Found in regions with other base metals like lead or copper, at moderate depths (100-300 meters).

9. Arsenic

    Use: Copper alloys, medicine, and poisoning (later in history).
    Mid Game:
        Found in mineralized veins in mountainous or volcanic areas, often alongside gold or silver ores.
        Found at mid to deep depths (50-200 meters).

** Professional Mana
Each pawn has a set amount of professional mana, which determines the amount of units it can occupy at once. Some units, which perform simple tasks, cost a low amount of professional mana, so the same pawn can choose to be several of these units. A pawn keeps working on a unit's task until there is nothing left to be done by that unit or if manually switched by the player or another unit's orders. Some units require all the professional mana a pawn has, and thus that pawn can only be that unit.

* Order
Determined by dimensions:
1. Legal
2. Administrative
3. Cultural
4. Satisfaction
5. Force

If Order is low, it is likely certain units will become criminals or rebels, killing other units, especially Leader units, and captured foreign units will be more likely to desert or rebel. It is also more likely the player will be aggressively attacked by other civilizations.

If Order is high, all units get a passive boost.
** Legal
This source of order is stable and really only hard to keep above a minimum level, or go down quickly, if High Leaders have recently been killed. However, it is hard for it to get to a high level. Raising Legal Order too high also causes Satisfaction Order to decrease, and makes it harder to produce Administrative Order.
- Produced in large amounts passively by High Leaders
- High Leaders have abilities to raise Legal Order immeadiately
- Produced by Priests, High Priests, Lawyers, Judges, Senators
- Senators can also ensure that Legal Order doesn't increase past a certain amount
- Reduced when New Knowledge is discovered
** Administrative
This source of Order depends on Legal Order. If Legal Order is too low or too high, Administrative Order will also be harder to create. As Legal Order goes up from a certain point, Administrative Order will also decrease, at a smaller rate. High Administrative Order also means that the Strength Order requirements are lower. Administrative Order can also be spent to enact certain Leader abilities, and is decently easy to create quickly. Low administrative order will also lower the amount of actual taxes that are collected, despite the set tax rate. Most importantly, Administrative Order affects Production Command.
- High Leaders produce large amounts passively, depending on their personality traits
- Produced by Landowner Subclasses, Bureaucrat, Senator, and Governor
- Raised by ample Royal wealth
- Lowered by large debt
** Cultural
This source of Order is difficult to create rapidly, and depends on many factors. High Cultural Order gives all Units a passive boost, and also unlocks certain Rituals which give further boosts or abilities. The only downside of having Cultural Order super high is that captured foreign units will have a larger debuff before they are assimilated.
- High Leaders produce an amount, depending on their personality traits
- Mostly produced by Art, especially Artifact Artists, which is multiplied based on the varieties, beauty level, and history of that artform in the nation.
- Also produced by Shamans, Priests, High Priests
- Modified positively or negatively by the amount of recently discovered Academic Knowledge
** Satisfaction
This source of Order is the easiest kind of Order to create rapidly, as long as the player has enough resources and units. However, if Satisfaction is quite low it is also likely that the player is lacking in other crucial resources. High Satisfaction also means that the Strength Order requirements are lower.
- Depends primarily on food supply, shelter supply
- Secondarily on food variety, art indulgence, art variety, shelter quality, and wealth.
- Tertiarily on environment beauty
- Lowered by large amounts of soldiers, and somewhat by large amounts of resource workers
- Lowered by high set taxes
- Hugely lowered by mass death, especially of civilians
** Strength
This source of Order is also quite difficult to relate because it is primarily based on army size and unit quality.
- Calculated by the total strength of army
- Also increased by landowners
* Knowledge
The forms of Knowledge are:
1. Exploration
2. Technology
3. Units
4. Foreign
5. Academic

New Knowledge of all forms will be unlocked passively at a certain rate (assuming certain units are doing other tasks), except Exploration, but the main source of Knowledge is Researchers. Knowledge itself, also affects other stats such as Cultural Order and Legal Order.

** Exploration
Explorers discover previously hidden tiles on the map which stay revealed, unlike other units, which will reveal them for a time and then fog of war will reappear. As explorers permanently reveal tiles, Exploration Knowledge increases. Since the world is infinite, Exploration Knowledge is always decreasing at a fixed rate. Maps from foreign nations can also be bought, which increases Exploration Knowledge and reveals areas of the map.
** Academic
This Knowledge functions more like an Order type. Academic Knowledge does nothing by itself, but vastly speeds up the production of other types of Knowledge and Order.

Perhaps the most important function of Academic Knowledge is that it can be used to create certain roles from Students through Trainers and books, and boost unit and technology research. There are also some technologies which are near impossible to unlock without a certain level of Academic research.

There are four subcategories of Academic Research:
1. Natural
2. Philosophy
3. Mathematics
4. History

The rate at which Academic Knowledge decays is lessened by the production of books.

AKA "Natural Philosophy", what we would think of as Science.

** Technology
The required tools which units need to do their roles. Sometimes, it will unlock a new role immediately (usually when a new level metal is discovered), but sometimes, the new roles need to be discovered independently of the technology they use (e.g: priest, scribe).

There is a small chance new technology can be invented by related classes, usually by the unit that evolves into the unit that uses or makes that technology, if the required resources to craft that technology are nearby. However, the most reliable way to unlock new technologies is by having a tech researcher near the required materials (which often means a large set of materials), where he will start to "work on" the problem, and then after a randomized amount of time will "discover" the new technology. Alternatively, technology researchers can be given related books instead when they become available, and given enough time will unlock related technologies.
** Units
Closely intertwined with technology, but often unlocking a new technology can unlock no units, one unit, or many units, and some units can be unlocked only ages after the technological requirements have been unlocked. The same discovery processes that apply to tech apply to units, but instead Researchers are assigned to follow around Units which are predecessors to the Unit that needs to be unlocked (though of course, unless the player has played the game before, they do not know what the unit is). Like technologies, researchers can also be given books.
** Foreign
This Knowledge allows closer relations with friendlies, or advantages in warfare against enemies. It also boosts Trade.

If a nation is an enemy (at war), only Spies can be sent, but if a nation is a friendly or neutral, all types of Foreign researchers can be sent. If a nation dislikes your nation, the ruler may or may not allow other Foreign Researchers.

If Foreign Research is high, it will also boost Academic, Technology, and Unit Research through Knowledge sharing, or Exploration Research by purchasing maps.

The amount of useful imports that are purchased by your nation. This number matters less, but the actual products you or your units are buying likely matters more. However, one the scores that affects it, /average savings/, likely does matter and determines whether you will make future imports. You can enact Royal Decrees to order quotas of specific imports, or leave it up to your traders based on your regular production quotas, if you have selected to "substitute imports for production".

Like Exports, this is an amalgam of /amount of imports/ and /average savings/.
**** Amount of Imports
The amount of imports you are buying. You can see this in a pie-chart view based on certain general classes of goods.
Affected by:
- Set Quotas by Royal Decrees
- Foreign Tarriffs placed by player
- Foreign Opinion
- Trade(Foreign) produced by Trader units
- Domestic Opinion of Foreign Nation
- Trade Deals/Embargos placed by player
- War/recent war
**** Average Savings
Affected by:
- Domestic goods production
- Amount of Imports (positively)
- Foreign Tarriffs placed by player
- Trade(Foreign) produced by Trader units
- Foreign Opinion
- Trade Deals

Amount of Profit made domestically. Increases taxes and Order(Satisfaction), or more precisely, the recreation hours needed to increase Order(Satisfaction). Also increases Order generally. Decreases Production Command. Decreases bartering time dramatically.

Affected by:
- Amount of Bankers
- Inflation
- Currency Coverage
- Production Command (negatively)

** Automatic Gear
Units have an inventory, wherein they can carry any type of item. They also have a gear, which is "equipped inventory". The player can control from a panel the default equipped state of each type of unit as a rule, which will globally make it so that units will automatically equip items to their gear that are set by the player if they are available and in their inventory. We use caching of checks, batch processing, and proximity-based checks to ensure minimal overhead, as well as lazy evaluation and event-driven between the inventory and the gear. This might also be something good to multithread. This can also apply to consumable items, i.e: when a carpenter runs out of wood, he will find more algorithmically.
** Items Lists
*** Required Items
Units have a required items list, which they require to even be that unit themselves. If a unit loses their required item, and they are already that unit, they will remain that unit, but will be unable to do work until they fulfill all the required items in their required items list.
*** Interactible Items List
Though every unit can haul items, most units can only equip/consume a small number of items.
*** Producible List
The list of items a unit can produce.

* Command
** Nation and Provinces
Overtime, provinces will start to develop. If a province has a governor, they can be given provincial decrees, which will give that province a boost of some kind. A similar national boost is available for the entire nation, which is determined by the Monarch at apporpriate times.
** Production Command
The player decides how much of each item and units to produce, and the units will affect their production based on this decree, and based on Order (Administrative), the actual units will affect the actual items they produce at this speed.
