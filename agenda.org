** Rivers and lakes
1. Identify Candidate Basins
   a. For each tile in the map:
       - If the tile is a local minimum (all neighbors higher or equal), mark as potential lake.
   b. Group connected local minima into basins (cluster adjacent low tiles).
   c. For each basin, compute:
       - Basin area = number of connected low tiles
       - Basin depth potential = minimum height difference between basin floor and lowest surrounding rim (lake height is randomized-- stored contiguosly with volume, and actual altitude lowers of volume reaches certain threshold due to player actions or seasons)
   d. Discard basins with depth < minimum threshold (e.g., 1-2 tiles)

2. Compute Water Input for Each Basin
   a. River inflow:
       - Sum volume of all rivers entering the basin (width × flow factor)
   b. Rainfall contribution:
       - rainfall_factor × basin_area
   c. Snowmelt contribution (optional):
       - Add seasonal snowmelt if basin contains mountain/snow tiles
   d. Total water input:
       - water_input = river_inflow + rainfall + snowmelt

3. Determine Lake Surface Area and Depth
   a. Compare water_input to basin capacity (max depth × basin area)
   b. Rules of thumb:
       - Low water_input → lake occupies only deepest tiles → small, deep lake
       - Medium water_input → lake fills most of basin → medium-sized, moderate depth
       - High water_input → lake fills basin, may overflow → large, shallow lake or floodplain

4. Connect Lakes to Rivers
   a. If a river enters the basin:
       - Add its flow to water_input
       - If water_input > basin capacity:
           - Create an outflow river at the lowest rim tile
   b. Ensure water flows logically downstream

5. Optional Seasonal Variation
   a. Wet season:
       - Expand lake into surrounding floodplain tiles
   b. Dry season:
       - Shrink lake, leaving only deepest tiles
   c. Adjust river inflow seasonally if desired

6. Optional Micro-Features
   a. Floodplains: 1–2 tile buffer around large/high-input lakes that fill in wet season
   b. Swamps/marshes: low-lying shallow areas with persistent water
   c. Sediment buildup: edges of lakes can gradually fill over time (optional)

7. Assign Lake Tile Properties
   a. Mark tiles as lake, floodplain, or wetland
   b. Assign depth (optional) based on tile elevation relative to lake water level
   c. Assign micro-features like vegetation, swamps, or marsh tiles

*** 1. Precompute Water Sources
**** Precipitation Map
- Assign each tile a base rainfall value.
- Apply seasonal multipliers:
  - Spring: 1.0
  - Summer: 1.1 (rainy regions) or 0.8 (dry regions)
  - Autumn: 0.6–0.8
  - Winter: 0.3–0.5

**** Snow/Glacier Map
- High-elevation tiles accumulate snow in winter.
- Snowmelt contributes to rivers based on season:
  - Spring: 100%
  - Summer: 20–50%
  - Autumn: 10–20%
  - Winter: 0–10%

**** Groundwater/Baseflow Map (optional)
- Slow, steady contribution from precipitation infiltrating soil.

*** 2. Determine River Sources
- Sources chosen from:
  - Mountain/high-altitude tiles with snow/glacier or high rainfall.
  - Groundwater-fed springs.
- Initial river volume or stage:
  - initial_flow = rainfall + snowmelt + groundwater

*** 3. River Path Generation
- Route rivers along lowest altitude / slope.
- Decide river age:
  - Young rivers → narrow, steep, greedy paths.
  - Mature rivers → wider, meandering, widen tiles along the path.
- Use chunk-based altitude approximation to reduce computation.

*** 4. Volume / Stage Propagation
**** Water Representation
- Float volume (continuous) or
- Discrete stages (integer levels, e.g., 0–10) like Minecraft water blocks.
  - Each stage corresponds to visual width/depth and flow potential.

**** Tile Update Formula
- tile_water = inflow_from_upstream + local_tile_input - evaporation - seepage
- Adjust river width/visuals based on volume/stage.

*** 5. Seasonal Updates
- Apply seasonal multipliers to water input:
  - Snowmelt: Spring 100%, Summer 20–50%, Autumn 10–20%, Winter 0–10%
  - Precipitation: Spring 1.0, Summer 1.1 or 0.8, Autumn 0.6–0.8, Winter 0.3–0.5
- Global seasonal river flow variation:
  - Spring: ~1.0× (peak)
  - Summer: 0.7–0.9×
  - Autumn: 0.6–0.8×
  - Winter: 0–0.5× (snow-fed rivers drop dramatically)
- Snow-fed rivers can vary 2–3× between peak and trough.
- Rain-fed rivers vary according to rainfall seasonality.

*** 6. Lake Generation
- Identify local minima in terrain.
- Determine lake type:
  - Small/floodplain → wet area, no major river.
  - Medium → connected to river, not wet area.
  - Large → connected to river and wet area.
- Update lake volume per season:
  - lake_volume_next = lake_volume_prev + inflow_from_rivers + local_tile_input - outflow - evaporation
- Depth effects:
  - Shallow lakes → fluctuate more, may dry out in dry season.
  - Deep lakes → more stable, smaller relative fluctuations.

*** 7. Optimizations for Tile-Based Games
- Use discrete stages instead of float volumes → reduces computation and simplifies overflow.
- Chunk-based pathfinding for river routing.
- Seasonal updates once per season instead of per frame.
- Overflow/spill: stage exceeds max → spill to adjacent lower tile.
- Width/visuals derived from volume/stage.

*** 8. Tile Water Inputs
- tile_water_input = rainfall + snowmelt + groundwater
- Seasonal multipliers applied.
- Drives initial river volume, propagation, and lake levels.

*** Outcome
- Rivers expand and shrink seasonally.
- Lakes store water and fluctuate realistically.
- Computation remains lightweight for tile-based games.
- Discrete stages simplify logic and rendering.

*** Architecture of Game System
- Cells/regions are spawned by the world spawner, which contain a variable amount of tiles which form various shapes as an entire cell when viewed from a bird's eye view.
  Each region has a biome, altitude, temperature, lakes, and rivers value. 

  They are deterministically spawned using a seed. The same seed is also used to create the vioronoi
